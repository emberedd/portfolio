Cost factors

What shapes the price

Every project is different, so I quote each one individually. These are the main things that move the number, for both camera work and editing.

Every project

  • Scope and deliverablesWhat you need delivered, in what format and how many pieces.
  • Timeline and turnaroundRush jobs and tight deadlines cost more than a flexible schedule.
  • Usage and licensingPersonal use, commercial use, paid ads or broadcast. Where it runs and for how long.
  • RevisionsHow many rounds of changes are included before extra edits are billed.
  • Location and travelFlights, hotels, mileage and time on the road for out-of-town work.
  • Schedule and availabilityPeak seasons, holidays and short-notice bookings.

Camera workPhoto and video

  • Shoot durationHourly or day rates, with a better rate on longer shoots.
  • Type of shootEvent, portrait, esports, promo and commercial work each carry different rates.
  • Number of shootersOne photographer or videographer, or a full crew with second shooters.
  • Photo and footage volumeHow many edited photos or finished clips, plus raw files if you want them.
  • EquipmentLighting, audio, drone or extra camera bodies beyond my standard kit.
  • Weather and conditionsOutdoor, rain or snow shoots can carry a premium.
  • Extra helpAssistants, makeup, stylists or additional crew.

Video editingPost-production

  • Final length and number of videosA 30-second promo and a 10-minute recap are very different jobs.
  • Amount of raw footageMore hours of footage means more time reviewing, selecting and cutting.
  • Style and complexityMontages, motion graphics, colour grading, sound design and captions.
  • Formats and versionsWidescreen, vertical and square cuts for different platforms.
  • Source materialWhether music, graphics and brand assets are supplied or need to be sourced.
  • Review roundsExtra revisions beyond the agreed number are billed on top.
  • Delivery speedSame-week turnarounds are priced differently than longer timelines.
